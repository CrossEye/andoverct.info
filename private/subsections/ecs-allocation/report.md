Making the ECS Grant Visible in the Andover Budget
==================================================

*A July 2026 letter asking the Board of Finance to allocate the state's
Education Cost Sharing grant between the two school appropriations and show
that allocation on the budget documents voters see, together with the sample
spreadsheets that demonstrate the presentation.*



The argument in one paragraph
-----------------------------

Andover receives about $2.08 million a year from the state in Education Cost
Sharing (ECS). The grant exists because the town runs an elementary school and
sends its older students to RHAM, yet the budget documents record it as
undifferentiated town revenue. Voters at the bifurcated referendum therefore see
the AES appropriation and the RHAM levy at their gross figures, with no sign of
the state money the schools generate. The letter asks the Board of Finance to
split the grant between AES and RHAM in proportion to the prior year's adopted
school budgets and to print that split, and the resulting net local cost, on
the face of the budget. Spending does not change. The mill rate does not change.
What changes is that a voter can see what each school budget actually costs the
town from the documents alone.



What the town does today
------------------------

The ECS grant is one line among the intergovernmental revenues on the town
side of the budget. For FY 2026-27 it is $2,084,974. The two school
appropriations, $4,839,390 for Andover Elementary and $4,121,131 for the RHAM
assessment, appear at their gross amounts. The grant reduces the amount to be
raised by taxes, but nothing in the documents connects it to either
appropriation.

This is the common practice in small towns with bifurcated budgets. Hebron's
budget message treats ECS the same way, and Marlborough appears to as well.
State law leaves the town free to account for the grant however it chooses;
there is no statutory requirement to credit it to education.



Why that is a problem
---------------------

The 2022 charter amendment split the budget so that voters could judge the town
budget, the AES budget and the RHAM levy each on its own terms. The current ECS
treatment works against that purpose:

- The gross school figures overstate the local burden the schools impose,
  because they exclude the state contribution that the schools themselves
  cause to exist.
- The $2.08 million reduction in the tax levy that ECS produces is attributed to
  nothing in particular. It is simply absorbed.
- A voter who wants to know whether the schools are funded at an appropriate
  level cannot work it out from the budget book.

When East Hampton's council applied most of a $700,000 ECS grant to general tax
relief in 2018, its finance director defended the move by saying the money had
in fact gone to education, since the education appropriation was met from a
mix of state aid and local taxes. That is technically true, and it concedes the
point: if the grant has been allocated to education, the allocation belongs on
the documents voters review, not in a verbal reconstruction after the fact.



The proposal
------------

Allocate each year's ECS grant between AES and the Andover RHAM levy in the same
proportions those two appropriations bore to one another in the prior fiscal
year, as adopted, and show the allocation and the resulting net local cost on
the budget documents. The letter asks for this to begin with the FY 2027-28
cycle.

The prior-year basis is the heart of the method. Because the proportions come
from adopted, audited figures, each side's ECS offset is a fixed dollar amount
from the day the state publishes the grant:

- It does not move when a proposed budget is revised between referendums. If one
  school budget passes while the other is still being cut, the passed budget's
  presentation is final and the other side's offset is unaffected.
- It avoids every contested per-pupil question: whether to count preschoolers,
  how to weight elementary against secondary students, how to treat outplaced
  students.
- It is self-adjusting. As the two school budgets move, the proportions follow a
  year behind, and the Board never has to revisit the formula.

The cost is a one-year lag in the proportions. In recent cycles the AES/RHAM
split has moved only a point or two a year, well within the tolerance of what
is an allocation convention rather than a measurement.

The letter is explicit that the method is a means, not the end. If the Board
prefers a different allocation formula, adopting any explicit allocation would
be fully responsive to the request.



What it would look like
-----------------------

The sample workbook re-presents the Board's own FY 2026-27 budget export of
June 2, 2026 with the ECS split added. Its FY 2026-27 column uses the FY 2025-26
adopted school budgets as the basis, which is what the rule would have used had
it been in force this year:

| FY 2026-27                      | Gross       | ECS allocated | Net local cost |
| ------------------------------- | ----------: | ------------: | -------------: |
| Andover Elementary (AES)        | $4,839,390  | $1,093,998    | $3,745,392     |
| RHAM assessment                 | $4,121,131  | $990,976      | $3,130,155     |
| **Education total**             | $8,960,521  | $2,084,974    | $6,875,547     |

Basis: FY 2025-26 adopted AES $4,565,462 and RHAM $4,135,532, giving AES 52.5
percent and RHAM 47.5 percent of the school total.

The letter itself illustrates the FY 2027-28 cycle, where the basis would be
the FY 2026-27 adopted figures above: AES 54.0 percent and RHAM 46.0 percent,
or $1,126,051 to AES and $958,923 to RHAM at this year's grant level.

The workbook's NetLocal sheet goes one step further and nets the town side too,
against its own fees, state aid and investment interest. That answers the
"one-sided" objection below: for FY 2026-27 the town side drops by about
$467,000 and the school side by $2.08 million, and the total to be raised by
taxes reconciles to the mill-rate sheet to the dollar.



Objections the letter anticipates
---------------------------------

- **"It's just bookkeeping; the tax bill is identical."** Yes. That is also the
  argument that a clearer presentation costs nothing.
- **"State law allows discretion."** Yes. The request is that the Board exercise
  that discretion in favour of transparency.
- **"If ECS falls, the schools bear it visibly."** The documents would show the
  fall where it belongs rather than absorbing it into generic mill-rate
  pressure. That is a feature.
- **"Per-pupil would be fairer."** Perhaps. If the Board can settle the
  preschool, weighting and outplacement questions, a per-pupil allocation would
  satisfy the request.
- **"Netting the schools but not the town is one-sided."** The fully symmetric
  version is in the workbook and the letter offers to supply it.
- **"Other towns don't do this."** Practice is mixed and there is no statewide
  convention. Andover can be more transparent than average, not less.

Status
------

The letter was sent on July 20, 2026. It asks the Board to place ECS allocation
practice on an upcoming agenda, and offers a five-minute presentation and a
comparison table of alternative methodologies if the Board wants one.
