1 Post 1 The money

Lead is text-only for this draft (Scott wants a card version on a later lap). Open the posted version with the public anchor: the demand letter, the meeting, or the news story that makes this public. Do not reveal any advance knowledge. The pattern -----------

Text only — no image for this post.

Something has come to light in Andover that residents should know about.

The First Selectman decided, on his own, to change how the elementary school
receives its own budgeted money. For two years the arrangement, agreed with the
Board of Finance, released the school one tenth of its budget each month. He
directed the treasurer to stop that, and instead to release the school's money
in $100,000 pieces, only after the school submits receipts justifying each
request.

There was no vote of the Board of Selectmen. There was no vote of the Board of
Finance. He did it by directive.

Set aside, for a moment, whether the school budget is a dollar too high or too
low. That question was settled at referendum. This is something different. This
is one official deciding that he will personally control when, and on what
terms, a school he does not run may spend the money the voters approved.

That is worth paying attention to. Because it did not come out of nowhere.

2 Post 2 The land

Needs a document: the record of the reassignment (a "what happened" beat).

Andover's Community and Senior Center sits on land the town owns but the school
district had administered for as long as anyone can remember.

Siting a senior center is a fair goal. A town needs one. The question was never
whether, it was how.

The First Selectman settled it by fiat. He reassigned the land from the school's
administration on his own say-so, rather than working it out with the Board of
Education the way a shared decision is normally reached.

The senior center is a good thing. The way the land was taken is a pattern worth
noticing.

3 Post 3 The solar project

Sourcing risk: the "normally negotiates directly with the board" claim is softened from "every other project in the state." Keep it to what you can show.

The elementary school worked for years toward putting solar panels on the
building, for the environmental benefit and to save money over time.

The company that runs these projects normally negotiates directly with a
school's board. The First Selectman insisted the town be made a party to the
school's agreement.

Adding the town to a deal it did not need to be part of slowed the negotiations
and produced nothing the project did not already have. A town has a real
interest in energy and in its buildings. That interest did not require inserting
itself between the school and a contract the school was capable of signing.

One more decision the school was allowed to make, until it wasn't.

4 Post 4 The preschool

His words plus the statutes. Good sourcing prospects: cite a specific statement and FERPA / IDEA.

For years the First Selectman has pressed the elementary school over its
preschool program, calling it a lack of transparency and raising the cost of
out-of-town students.

Asking what a program costs is fair. Here is what the answers actually are.

The data he calls hidden is student information the school is required to protect
under federal privacy law, FERPA and the special-education law IDEA. The school
is not concealing it. The school is complying.

And the out-of-town students are not a drain. They cannot be placed here for
special education at Andover's expense, and they pay full tuition, which is more
than the cost of adding them to a class the town is already running.

A fair question deserves a real answer. The answer keeps being given. The
question keeps being asked as if it had not been.

5 Post 5 The salary

Ties to the 45-district peer study. This beat becomes a number / stat card once the per-FTE figure and rank are in; text for now.

The First Selectman has pushed to cut the superintendent's salary.

Scrutinizing what public leaders are paid is legitimate. So look at the number.

Measured against comparable districts, adjusted for the size of the district she
runs, Andover's superintendent is the lowest paid in her peer group. Not near
the bottom. The bottom.

You can argue a public salary is too high. It is harder to argue that the
lowest-paid superintendent in her peer group is the place to find savings.
Unless the salary was never really the point.

6 Post 6 Every table, every session

Grievance-prone. The executive-session and any records-request details are the ones to verify hardest against the public record, or cut. Meeting minutes show the attendance pattern; keep the posted copy to what the record supports.

Andover's charter lets the First Selectman sit as an ex officio member of town
boards. For as long as anyone can recall, no one used it that way. The work was
done by a liaison who attended, listened, and reported back.

This First Selectman uses it differently. He has made himself a fixture at the
Finance and Education boards, has pressed to sit in on their executive sessions,
the closed portions meant for sensitive matters, and treats the school budget as
his standing agenda item wherever he goes.

A coordinating role means a board keeps informed of what the others are doing.
It does not mean one official sits inside every closed-door discussion and drives
every meeting back to the same target.

The role was there for decades and no one needed to use it like this. Ask
yourself why this is the moment it became indispensable.

7 Post 7 And there's more

The "as well" list, with the bathroom concession stated plainly. Keep the concession as its own clean sentence; it is the credibility move. The price ---------

Not every instance is the same size, and one deserves a fair word.

The bathroom renovation is a $325,000 project. On that one, the First Selectman
had a point. The way the school handles small repairs, letting its engineering
firm price the work and act as general contractor, is reasonable for a minor job
and a fair question for a project that large. That criticism was legitimate.

The rest belong to the same pattern. He questioned the contracting the school
had used for years. He spent his own money on town-wide mailers urging residents
to vote down the school's budget. And he has made the school a recurring target,
meeting after meeting, board after board.

One fair point among many. The fair point does not explain the rest.

8 Post 8 Who pays for this

Turnover is documentable; attribution stays careful ("in the same period," not "because of him"). Add the turnover count once you have it; could become a number card. The turn --------

Step back from any single dispute and look at the cost of all of it.

In the last eighteen months, Andover Town Hall has seen heavy turnover among its
staff. In that same period, the superintendent, who once came before the Board
of Selectmen a handful of times a year, has had to attend most of its meetings,
answering the same objections raised again and again.

That is time and attention a small town cannot spare, spent not on running a
school or a town but on one official's effort to control both.

Whatever you think of any one decision, this is what a pattern costs.

Land taken by fiat. A solar project slowed for no gain. A privacy law recast as
