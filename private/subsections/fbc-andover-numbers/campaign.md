Facebook Campaign: Andover's 2025-26 Results (Sept 2026)
========================================================

Six standalone posts over a week or so, celebrating Andover Elementary's spring 2026 results without overselling them. No through-line and no signposting: each post stands on its own, and the honest footnote at the end is what earns the rest their credibility.

All copy below is paste-ready. Every post links back to the-facts edition, which carries the charts, the sourcing and the full workbook.



### Day one - The headline ###

Image: `01_headline.png`

The strongest single fact, stated plainly. Lead the week with it.

```
Andover Elementary just posted the largest one-year gain of any town school district in Connecticut. Not in one subject. In both.

On the state's performance index, Andover's English score rose 6.1 points and its math score rose 6.8. Out of 164 town districts, no one else gained more in either subject.

The state as a whole moved 0.3 points in English and 0.6 in math.

The numbers, the charts and where they come from:
https://andoverct.info/the-facts/andover-numbers-2026/
```



### Day two - Against the state average ###

Image: `02_proficiency.png`

```
Another way to look at the same results: how many students actually met the standard.

In reading, 73% of Andover students did, against 51% across Connecticut. In math, 69% against 47%.

About 110 Andover students in grades 3 through 6 sat these tests in the spring.

https://andoverct.info/the-facts/andover-numbers-2026/
```

### Day three - The fairer measure ###

Image: `03_growth.png`

Growth is the measure least explained by demographics, which is why it is worth its own post.

```
Test scores mostly tell you which students a district serves. There is a better measure, and Andover's is remarkable.

Connecticut gives every student an individual growth target based on their own prior score, whether they are behind or already ahead, then asks how much of that target they met.

Andover students met 85% of their targets on average this year. The state figure was 60%. A year ago Andover was at 69%.

That is the number that is hardest to explain away by who a district teaches.

https://andoverct.info/the-facts/andover-numbers-2026/
```

### Day five - The obvious objection ###

Image: `05_not_demographics.png`

Meets the most likely pushback head-on rather than waiting for it.

```
A fair question about any jump in test scores: did the students change, or did the teaching?

Across Connecticut, the single strongest predictor of a district's scores is the share of its students with high needs. Andover's share did fall slightly this year, so some of the gain comes from that.

But only some. Adjusting every district in the state for student need, 4.1 points of Andover's 5.5-point combined gain are still there.

Most of it is not demographics.

https://andoverct.info/the-facts/andover-numbers-2026/
```



### Day six - The honest footnote ###

Image: `06_one_good_year.png`

Closes the run on the school's own caution. Do not sand this one down; it is what makes the other five trustworthy.

```
Three things worth saying alongside the good news.

Science went the other way, down 3.3 points. It is tested in one grade here, so it rests on about two dozen students and swings hard in both directions.

About 110 students sit these tests. A strong year group moves the whole district figure.

And one year is one year. The principal said it better than we could:

"It is not a one-shot deal. We can't just say we had one good year."

The full picture, including the parts that complicate it:
https://andoverct.info/the-facts/andover-numbers-2026/
```
