A Question for Five Candidates
==============================

One question went to five Republican candidates on our ballots. One of them answered. The run follows what came back, and what did not.

All copy below is paste-ready. The two cards carrying "as of" dates (03_weir, 04_three) need regenerating if the run slips more than a few days.


Day one - the question
----------------------


### Day one - The question ###

Image: `01_question.png`

The disclosure paragraph is deliberate. Better that I say it first than have someone else say it for me.

```
On September 10 I sent the same question, in the same words, to four Republican candidates who will be on my ballot in November. The next day I sent it to a fifth.

Will you publicly disavow Donald Trump?

By that I meant a statement in their own name that he does not represent them and does not have their support. Not a comment on a single policy. Not a statement about the party in general.

I promised each of them the same thing. Whatever they send me gets published in full and unedited, whether I like it or not, with the date it arrived. If they would rather answer somewhere else, in the Rivereast or on their own page, I will link it. If they have already answered somewhere and I missed it, I will say so.

I should say plainly that I co-chair the Andover Democratic Town Committee. This is my own project and not the committee's, and the page says so at the top of it. Weigh that however you like. Every letter is published word for word, so nobody has to take my summary of anything.

The record is here, and it updates as answers come in:
andoverct.info/reports/ahm/disavowal/
```


Day two - the one who answered
------------------------------


### Day two - Credit where it is due ###

Image: `02_jennings.png`

```
Jenn Jennings, the Republican running for state Senate in the 4th District, answered in three days.

Her answer was no.

"No sir, I will not, past, present or future."

She went further than that. She told me she thought it was the wrong question to put to a candidate for the state Senate, that Americans were not asked to denounce the last president and should not be asked to denounce this one, and that defining people by which figures they will denounce leaves very little room for disagreement or common ground.

I disagree with her, and I wrote back the same evening to say so and to explain why I asked. Both letters are on the page.

But she took a question she plainly did not like, answered it in her own name, on the record, knowing it would be published, and did it inside of three days. That is more than most candidates manage with a question they would rather duck.

andoverct.info/reports/ahm/disavowal/
```


Day three - our own representative
----------------------------------


### Day three - Steve Weir ###

Image: `03_weir.png`

The sober wording here is the point. If I overclaim, he gets to answer the overclaim instead of the question.

```
Steve Weir is my state representative, in the 55th District.

He was sent the question on September 10, at the campaign address on his own filing with the State Elections Enforcement Commission. The same letter went to his legislative address, the one his office staffs, because I am one of his constituents.

There has been no reply.

I want to be careful here, because the page itself is careful. Silence is not proof of anything. It is a statement about my inbox on a given date and nothing more. He may answer tomorrow. If he does, it goes up in full, unedited, exactly like everyone else's.

But another Republican on my own ballot answered in three days, in a single sentence, and I published it the same week. It is not a hard question to answer. It is only a hard question to avoid.

andoverct.info/reports/ahm/disavowal/
```


Day four - the others
---------------------


### Day four - The other three ###

Image: `04_three.png`

```
Three others were asked the same question on the same terms.

Ryan Fazio, running for governor. Jason Guidone, running for state Senate in the 19th District. George Austin, the Republican nominee for Congress in the 2nd District, who was asked on September 11.

Nothing back from any of them yet.

Worth saying plainly: this is a question about the man, not about the party. Every one of these candidates runs under his banner because he leads their party. That is a fact about the ballot, not an accusation about the candidate. What each of them actually thinks of him is something only that candidate can supply.

Any one of them can end this with one sentence, and I will publish it exactly as written, the same day it arrives.

andoverct.info/reports/ahm/disavowal/
```
