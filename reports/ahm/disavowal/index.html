A Question for Four Candidates

Connecticut 2026 · Governor, Senate 4, Senate 19, House 55 · One question asked of four Republican candidates, and a public record of the answers

A personal project by Scott Sauyet · scott.sauyet@gmail.com · I co-chair the Andover Democratic Town Committee; this page is my own work and not an official document of any committee, town, or campaign

On September 10, 2026 I sent the same question, in the same words, to four Republican candidates on Connecticut ballots this November. This page records who was asked, at what address, on what date, and what each has said in response. Any answer I receive will be published here in full and unedited, whether I like it or not, with the date it arrived. Contact addresses come from each committee’s registration with the State Elections Enforcement Commission or from the campaign’s own published contact page, and both are cited below. Where this page says no answer has been received, that is a statement about my inbox on the date shown, and nothing more. I am a Democrat and I co-chair the Andover Democratic Town Committee. I am asking anyway, and readers can weigh that as they see fit.

Introduction

Overview¶

I have one question for the Republicans asking for my vote this year, and I would rather put it to them directly than guess at the answer. The question is whether they will publicly disavow Donald Trump: a statement in their own name that he does not represent them and does not have their support.

That is not a question about a policy, a bill, or a vote in Hartford. It is a question about the head of the party whose banner every one of these candidates runs under, and every one of them has had a decade to decide what they think of him.

Four candidates were asked, on the same day, in the same words. Three of them appear on my own ballot in Andover. The fourth lives in a town that shares a House district with mine. All four are participants in Connecticut’s Citizens’ Election Program with grants awarded, meaning each asked the public to finance his or her campaign, and the public agreed.

This page is the record. It will be updated as answers come in, through Election Day on November 3, 2026. There is no deadline. An answer at any point gets added.

The ask

The question, as sent¶

The body of all four emails was the same but for the salutation, the sentence identifying the district, and one paragraph noted below. The question itself read:

Will you publicly disavow Donald Trump? By that I mean a statement in your own name that he does not represent you and does not have your support. Not a comment on a single policy, and not a statement about the party in general.

Each letter disclosed that I co-chair the Andover Democratic Town Committee, offered to publish the reply in full and unedited, offered to link an answer given anywhere else instead, and invited correction if the candidate had already answered publicly and I had missed it.

One paragraph appeared only in the letter to Sen. Fazio, noting that he has discussed his own past votes for Mr. Trump and has said he would welcome Mr. Trump’s endorsement, and that the question here is whether he will disavow him now.

Standard

Part 3 — What counts as an answer¶

Every reply will be classified, and the classification stated plainly beside it, on this scale:

Disavowal¶

An unambiguous statement in the candidate’s own name that Mr. Trump does not represent them and does not have their support.

Criticism¶

Objection to a specific act, decision, or policy, without withdrawal of support. This is a real answer and will be published as one. It is not a disavowal, and will not be described as one.

Deflection¶

The question was answered with something else: a pivot to state issues, a statement that the candidate does not agree with everything the President does, or a reply about the party rather than the man.

Support¶

A statement that the candidate does support Mr. Trump. This is also a real answer, and an honest one. It will be published in the same words and given the same prominence as any other.

No response¶

Nothing received. Reported as of a date, never as a permanent condition.

The count is the weak version of the finding. The stronger version is what the advertising does discuss. Sen. Fazio’s ads return repeatedly to immigration and federal enforcement, at length and in his own voice, and reach for the phrase “federal authorities” rather than the name of the President who directs them. The single instance of the word “administration” in nearly an hour of recorded audio refers to Gov. Lamont’s.

Mr. Guidone came through a contested Republican primary between February and August of this year, ran thirty-one ads, and criticized his opponent by reference to his record and his tenure without either man’s advertising touching the President.

None of that is an accusation. It is the reason the question is worth asking rather than assuming.

Precedent

Part 5 — Whether the ask is a fair one¶

A reasonable objection is that no Republican could answer this question and survive a primary, so asking it is a trap rather than an inquiry. The record suggests otherwise.

Themis Klarides led the Republican caucus in the Connecticut House from 2015 to 2021. During her 2022 campaign for the United States Senate she said she had not voted for Mr. Trump in 2020, casting a protest write-in for her predecessor as caucus leader instead. She said it in her own name, in public, while seeking her party’s endorsement, and she won that endorsement on the first ballot with about 57 percent of the delegates. She has never reversed it.

In December of that year, two former Republican leaders of the state Senate, Len Fasano and John McKinney, published an opinion piece in the Hartford Courant arguing that the party “needs to reject” Mr. Trump as a candidate.

Two things should be said plainly about those examples. All three were former officeholders rather than sitting ones when they spoke, and all three statements date to 2022. No sitting Connecticut Republican legislator has made a comparable statement in 2025 or 2026. The ask is answerable, and it has been answered before, but not recently and not by anyone currently in office.

Takeaways

Key Observations¶

The question is about the man, not the party. Every candidate here runs under Mr. Trump’s banner because he leads their party. That is a fact about the ballot, not an accusation about the candidate. What each of them thinks of him is something only that candidate can supply.

Being asked and being accused are different things. Nothing on this page asserts that any of these four candidates supports Mr. Trump, admires him, or has ever said a word about him. The page records a question and whatever comes back.

Silence is not evidence. A blank row means the mail has not arrived. Voters can decide for themselves what to make of an unanswered question, and different voters will reasonably decide differently.

The public is paying for these campaigns. All four candidates take part in the Citizens’ Election Program and have been awarded grants. That confers no legal obligation to answer anyone’s questions, and this page claims none. It does mean the campaigns are publicly financed, and a voter who notices that is not being unreasonable.

An answer ends the matter, whatever it says. A candidate who replies “I support him,” in those words, has answered honestly and will be recorded as having done so, in the same type as everyone else.

Corrections are welcome and will be made quickly. If a candidate has already answered this question somewhere I have not looked, the correction belongs on this page more than the omission does.

Limits

What this report does not show¶

It does not show that any candidate has never discussed Mr. Trump. Only paid advertising was collected. Organic posts on the candidates’ own Facebook pages, their remarks at forums and debates, their interviews, and their answers to other people’s questions were not systematically searched, and any of those could contain a statement this page has missed.

It does not show that the question was received, read, or seen by the candidate. Email to a campaign address is not certified mail.

It does not show that a non-answer is a considered choice. Campaigns are busy, inboxes overflow, and a message can be lost without anyone deciding anything.

It does not show anything about candidates who were not asked. Four Republicans were written to because they appear on my ballot or in my House district. Others on Connecticut ballots this year were not, and their absence from this page means only that.

It is not a neutral document. I wrote it, I am a Democrat, and I would like these candidates to answer. What is offered here is not the absence of a point of view but a record specific enough that a reader who disagrees with me can check every part of it.
