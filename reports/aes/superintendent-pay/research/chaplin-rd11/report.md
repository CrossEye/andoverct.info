Chaplin / Regional District 11 Shared Central Office
====================================================

Background memo for the superintendent-pay comparison. Chaplin was the structural
oddball in the 45-district elementary set: it shares its entire central office
(including the superintendent) with Regional School District 11, so its
"superintendent salary" is a negotiated fraction of a shared position rather than
a clean single-district number. This memo documents exactly how that split works
and what the FY 2025-26 numbers are, sourced to the adopted budgets.



Bottom line
-----------

Chaplin Elementary School (CES) and Regional School District 11 (Parish Hill
Middle/High School, PHMS/HS) run one shared central office governed by a Central
Office Committee (COC). The COC adopts a single central-office budget each year
and splits every function between the two districts by a negotiated,
function-specific percentage. For FY 2025-26 the total COC budget is $1,006,485,
divided $485,479 to Chaplin (about 48%) and $521,005 to RD 11 (about 52%).

The split is not one blanket percentage. Each office carries its own ratio:

- Superintendent's Office: 40% Chaplin / 60% RD 11
- Business Office: 40% Chaplin / 60% RD 11
- Special Education Office: 50% Chaplin / 50% RD 11
- Maintenance: 25% Chaplin / 75% RD 11 (per the "MAINTENANCE-25/75" object label)
- Technology / IT Director: roughly 80% Chaplin / 20% RD 11

For superintendent-pay purposes: the superintendent's salary line in the COC
budget is $141,750 for FY 2025-26, split $56,700 to Chaplin (40%) and $85,050 to
RD 11 (60%).



How the two districts relate
----------------------------

The shared office spans two legally distinct school systems:

- **Chaplin Elementary School (CES)**, PK through 6, is the town of Chaplin's own
  district, overseen by a seven-member elected Chaplin Board of Education and
  funded through the town budget.
- **Regional School District 11 (RD 11)** operates Parish Hill Middle/High School
  (grades 7-12) in Chaplin and serves the three member towns of Chaplin, Hampton,
  and Scotland. RD 11 has its own board (three members per town) and its own
  budget approved by tri-town referendum.

Chaplin is the common thread: it is both a member town of RD 11 and the operator
of its own elementary school, and the two systems share one administration.



The Central Office Committee and how costs are split
----------------------------------------------------


### The governing document ###

The authoritative allocation schedule is the **Central Office Committee Budget
25-27**, COC-approved March 19, 2025. It is not published as a standalone file,
but it is embedded (pages 26 onward) inside Chaplin's FY 2025-26 Revised Budget
Package. The same object-309 "CENTRAL OFFICE" charges also appear, split out by
function, in each district's own adopted budget: the RD 11 side in the RD 11
FY 2025-26 budget, the Chaplin side in the Chaplin budget package.

The COC budget presents each central-office function with four year-columns
(2022-23 audited through 2025-26) followed by two allocation columns showing the
Chaplin dollar share and the RD 11 dollar share, plus a plain-language annotation
(for example "40% Chaplin") on the lead salary line of each office.


### Per-function allocation percentages, FY 2025-26 ###

| Central-office function        | Chaplin | RD 11 |
|:-------------------------------|--------:|------:|
| Superintendent's Office        |     40% |   60% |
| Business Office                |     40% |   60% |
| Special Education Office       |     50% |   50% |
| Maintenance (Object 114)       |     25% |   75% |
| Technology / IT Director       |    ~80% |  ~20% |

The maintenance ratio is baked into the object label itself ("MAINTENANCE-25/75").
The technology ratio is inferred from the two sides' 309 charges (Chaplin
$118,296 vs. RD 11 $29,573), so treat it as approximate pending the COC line
detail. The differing per-function ratios are why the aggregate split lands near
48/52 even though the two biggest offices are 40/60.

What this means for the superintendent-pay comparison
-----------------------------------------------------

Chaplin does not employ a stand-alone superintendent. The person is shared with
RD 11 (and, per press coverage, also serves Hampton Elementary). Three different
numbers could each be the "right" one depending on the methodology:

1. **Full superintendent salary as carried in the COC budget: $141,750.** This is
   the figure most comparable to another district's superintendent salary, but it
   covers the Chaplin-plus-RD 11 workload only.
2. **Chaplin's attributed share: $56,700 (40%).** This is what the town of Chaplin
   actually pays toward the superintendent. It understates the compensation of the
   person, but correctly states Chaplin's cost.
3. **Full central-office cost, not just salary.** If the comparison is about
   administrative overhead rather than one salary, the relevant Chaplin figure is
   the $485,479 total 309 charge, or the $102,837 Chaplin superintendent's-office
   share including benefits and support staff.

The cleanest footnote for the cross-district table is probably: Chaplin shares a
superintendent with RD 11 on a 40/60 basis; the superintendent's salary is
$141,750, of which Chaplin bears $56,700. Then pick whichever of those two numbers
matches how the other 44 districts are counted.

One open question flagged below: whether $141,750 is the superintendent's full
contractual salary or only the Chaplin-plus-RD 11 portion, since the same person
reportedly also superintends Hampton Elementary.



History of the arrangement
--------------------------

The central-office compact once covered the elementary schools of all three RD 11
towns (Chaplin, Hampton, Scotland) plus the regional secondary school. The Hampton
and Scotland elementary boards withdrew from the central-office compact in the
early 2000s, leaving today's two-party arrangement between Chaplin Elementary and
RD 11 / Parish Hill.

In 2016-2017 a formally constituted Dissolution Committee studied dissolving RD 11
outright and issued a Final Report in 2017 (with appendices on member-town
start-up costs and a State Board of Education letter of action). The region was not
dissolved, and the shared-office structure has continued.



Distinction from the town-assessment formula
---------------------------------------------

Do not conflate the two different "splits" in play:

- The **central-office split** (this memo) divides the shared administration
  between two school systems, CES and RD 11/PHHS, by the function-specific
  percentages above.
- The **town-assessment formula** divides the RD 11 operating budget ($7,275,881
  for FY 2025-26) among the three member towns (Chaplin, Hampton, Scotland) by a
  separate enrollment-based assessment under the regional-district statutes. That
  is a town-to-town cost allocation, not a CES-to-PHHS one.

A number pulled from the wrong one of these will not reconcile.



Open items to verify
--------------------

- Whether the $141,750 superintendent salary is the full contractual figure or
  only the Chaplin-plus-RD 11 share, given the reported Hampton Elementary role.
  If Hampton pays a separate share, total superintendent compensation exceeds the
  COC figure.
- The exact technology/IT and buildings/maintenance percentages, which differ from
  the 40/60 pattern and were partly inferred here from the two sides' 309 charges.
- The superintendent's actual employment contract, if the comparison needs total
  compensation (benefits, annuities, expense allowances) rather than the salary
  line. Contracts are public under CT Public Act 17-2 via the town clerk.
- The apparent 2023-24 to 2024-25 salary jump, to confirm whether it reflects a
  superintendent transition.
