RHAM Region 8 · Decade trend

RHAM Per-Pupil Spending in Context

Eleven years of Net Current Expenditures Per Pupil for RHAM and the seven other Connecticut regional districts that serve only secondary grades, with comparison against CPI-U inflation and the Social Security COLA. The headline +96.6% per-pupil increase over the decade is among the steepest in the peer group, but is overwhelmingly a denominator effect: enrollment fell 36.5% while total operating spending grew slower than inflation. Despite that, RHAM still ranks 6th of 8 on per-pupil spending in 2024–25.

Andover, Hebron & Marlborough →

Andover · Hebron · Marlborough · RHAM · FY 2013–FY 2023

Spending, Students, and Per-Pupil Costs

Ten years of federal school-finance data (NCES/Census F-33) for Andover, Hebron, Marlborough, and RHAM, with maps and charts for every group examined. Combined spending for the four districts grew 17% over the decade while inflation rose 29.5% — yet per-pupil cost climbed 68%, because enrollment fell 30%. The report widens the lens step by step, from the core towns through three contiguous rings of neighbors to 45 elementary-only peer districts and the state as a whole, showing the same denominator arithmetic at every scale.

Four Years of Bills

Every one of the 90 bills Rep. Steve Weir (R-55) introduced or cosponsored across his four sessions, 2023 through 2026, with the disposition of each resolved from the Connecticut legislative datasets and confirmed against the chaptered public acts. He put his name at the top of 41 bills and none became law; 24 he filed alone died in committee without a hearing. His name is nonetheless on 33 enacted laws, all as a cosponsor of committee bills. Steve Weir's Hidden Business: What the Public Record Shows

CT 55th House District · Roll-call analysis

$630,000 in Town Aid and One Vote Against It

A walkthrough of Rep. Steve Weir's (R-55) May 2, 2026 vote on SB 1, the FY 2026–27 budget adjustment that directs roughly $270 million in new K-12 and municipal aid to Connecticut towns. Using the Lamont administration's town-by-town breakdown, the report estimates approximately $630,000 of that aid is headed to the five towns of the 55th. The bill passed 127–21; Weir voted no. Documents that the vote was not party-line — 26 of 47 voting Republicans voted yes, including neighbors from the 8th District — and points readers to Weir's own statements for the reasoning.

CT 55th House District · Voting-record analysis

Where Weir Sits in the House

Where Rep. Steve Weir (R-55) falls on the ideological spectrum of the Connecticut House, and how that compares to the partisan lean of the district he represents. Every member is placed on a single liberal-to-conservative scale built from their recorded roll-call votes using W-NOMINATE, a standard spatial method, for the 2025–2026 term and, for comparison, 2023–2024. Each district's partisan lean is a recency- and turnout-weighted average of its towns' two-party vote for President, U.S. Senate, U.S. House, and Governor across the 2012–2024 general elections. The findings are stable across both of Weir's terms: he votes among the most conservative members of the House, to the right of his own party's leader, while the 55th leans Democratic in statewide and federal elections.

Connecticut →

Connecticut vs. the nation · 26 indicators

The Whole Ledger

Connecticut measured against the rest of the country on 26 indicators at roughly 2010, 2019, and the latest available year — the span of unified state government — with every figure traced to a primary source (Census, BEA, BLS, EIA, FBI, NCHS/CDC, NCES and others) and checked against it before publication. The answer is genuinely mixed and the mix has a shape: the clearest gains are fiscal (a rainy day fund rebuilt from zero to its statutory cap, pension funding rising, a credit rating recovered through eight upgrades), the clearest strengths are inherited quality-of-life leads the rest of the country is steadily closing, and the clearest failures are costs and growth — taxes, electricity, housing production, population, and an overdose crisis that flipped Connecticut from better than the nation to worse.
